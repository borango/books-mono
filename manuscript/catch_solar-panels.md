## Solar Panels

They [catch](#pp02) [sunlight](#sunlight), an endless renewable resource, and turn it into electric energy.

Together with a modern charge controller, they stop charging when batteries are full and resume charging when in demand, which is an example of [self-regulation](#pp04).

A great opportunity on a boat, even better on a catamaran, is the available space for mounting solar panels. Your first choice is to Install walkable, flexible, "flush-mount" solar panels on horizontal surfaces: cabin roofs and deck. This follows the same logic as putting panels on roofs of houses at land. You want to [integrate, not segragate](#pp08). A solar farm on the ground looks way less natural than on roof tops.

How much solar panels you need on an Eco sailboat is a matter of your energy demand, which I discuss in thei [electro backbone](#electro-backbone) chapter. Together with the [wind turbine](#wind-turbine) and a recuperating [engine](#engine) or other "hydro generator"[^hydro_generator_misnomer], solar panels must deliver the supply for all of your electric energy demand.

[^hydro_generator_misnomer]: misnomer; this device does not generate water but generates electricty from motion through water

On land you can often scale up your solar panel installation to meet your preferred demand. On the limited space of your boat, you typically calclate backwards and scale down your habits until your reduced needs match the available supply of energy.

Therefore most likely you want to:

> **install all the solar panels that you possibly can**

If you have a fixed bimini top installed, then this is the next best place [^bimini_next_best_place] for solar panels. In this case they should be rigid, because a soft bimini top does not provide enough structural strength to support flexible panels.

[^bimini_next_best_place]: Personally I don't like a bimini top above the helm, because it separates me from the main sail. It is in my way for reefing or dropping the sail.

I do not recommend to construct a new roof-like structure for the solar panels, for aerodynamic reasons. What seems to be horizontal at rest will be partially vertical when the boat is leaning or rolls in parallel waves. I assume that you do not want an extra mizzen sail, and in particular not when winds are strong or gusty. It makes your boat more weather helm (increases its tendency to head up into the wind) and increases rudder force when you least need it. At the very least, it changes the balance in your sail plan and moves the wind's center of effort further aft.

If you already have a fixed bimini top, then the aerodynamic damage is already done and you can just as well add solar panels to it. I suggest that you do not add panels on top of your davits.

If you need to resolve the conflict between energy supply ("install all you can") and safety in a storm, I suggest that you keep the structure of your boat 
clean and safe for storms, and rather use mobile or foldable solar panels in absolutely calm conditions. You can recharge your batteries and even sail in a calm with mobile panels which are strapped to the deck or to the reling, when conditions allow it.

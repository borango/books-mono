# Tools

When you live aboard fulltime there is no natural break, like the winter season, when you stop sailing and start reparing. In a nomadic boatlife you should be prepared to maintain and repair your floating home on the go. That includes the boat itself with its traditional components: hull and rig. And also to renewable components like the electric installation.

The more you can fix on board, the less you depend on infrastructure on land. It increases your autonomy.

In many cases that requires also to learn new skills like sailmaking (well, sawing thick stuff), splicing, carpentry, painting, filling and glassing with epoxy, or working with electronics. Learning new skills is kind of a habit by itself.

Here I am listing some of the tools and spares that I find handy to have on the boat.

## extra rope

... of all diameters and types that you already have on board, for replacement, and also for new installations

## shackles

... of all diameters which make sense on your size of boat (up to 8, 10 or 12mm). Quick shackles of various sizes.

I find climbing carabiners a great solution for many improvised solutions. Some of the well anodized ones tolerate the saltwater very well and other just corrode.

Soft shackles are a great alternative, when abrasion is not an issue. Splicing your own soft shackle from dyneema is simple and inexpensive.

## blocks

... for all sizes of your standard rig (sheets, halyards) and also smaller ones.

Where friction is no problem, aluminium so-called "low friction" rings and similar one-piece-blocks can be a lighter and durable solution.

Also consider making traditional blocks from hardwood.

## wood

hardwood for smaller parts like spacers, re-enforcement or blocks, and also larger boards

## splicing tool

for rope and soft shackles to be used in the rigging

## (power) drill

... and a large selection of bolts

## saw

... and the electric version, a multi tool. Can cut in the most amazing angles and corners.

## angle grinder

... for the heavy duty cutting or for quickly sanding down a spot

## welding machine

they come also in mini format

## marine sealant

... along with its caulking gun. It should always be charged and ready to use, so that you don't postpone smaller repairs

## soldering iron

... and cable of all diameters.

Here it is helpful to have standard levels of current on board, such as 20 Amps, which you can always safely install with the same wire cross-section.

Also the fitting wire terminators (e.g. ring terminators) and a crimping tool.

## lab power supply

To emulate all power levels that you might work with, to set safe limits of voltage or current, and to measure the actual power being drawn by a component.

I recommend one that goes up to your maximum DC charging voltage, which could be < 60 Volts. Higher power such as 1 kW also makes it a capcable battery charger, should you be connected to shore power.
